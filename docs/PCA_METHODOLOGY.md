# PCA Methodology

The dashboard uses PCA twice, both in the **Analytics (PCA)** view (key `6` or `A`; `AnalyticsTabContent.jsx`), which has two modes:

- **PCA Biplot** (the cross-race PCA, `PCABiplot.jsx`, with the Cross-Race Analysis panel `PCAAnalysisPanel.jsx` beside it): one point per (driver, race) of the cached races, fitted in the backend (`backend/data/processors/pca_compute.py`);
- **Race Situation** (the live PCA, `RaceSituationMap.jsx`, with the Strategy Analysis panel `StrategyAnalysis.jsx` beside it; also a view of its own in the dock): one point per driver at the current lap of the race on screen, fitted in the browser every lap (`frontend/src/utils/livePCA.js`).

Every number below is a script output saved under `docs/evidence/` (file named next to it; index: `docs/evidence/README.md`). The scripts:

| Output | Script (run from `backend/` or `frontend/`) |
|---|---|
| `pca_rebuild.txt` | `python -m scripts.pca_rebuild --out ../docs/evidence/pca_rebuild.txt` (also rewrites the two artefacts below) |
| `pca_diagnostic.txt` | `python -m scripts.pca_diagnostic --out ../docs/evidence/pca_diagnostic.txt` |
| `pca_clock_check.txt`, `pca_interval_coverage.txt`, `pca_selection_baseline.txt`, `pca_stratified_baseline.txt`, `pca_mixed_threshold.txt`, `pca_progress_features.txt` | `python -m scripts.<name> --out ../docs/evidence/<name>.txt` |
| `pca_repair_check.txt`, `live_pca_rewind.txt` | `npx vite-node scripts/<name>.mjs` (from `frontend/`) |

## 1. The cross-race feature space

Each point is one (driver, race) row of the cached race sessions (`backend/data/processed_cache/session_*_R.json`). A row enters the matrix when the driver completed at least 90% of the race leader's laps and has at least 5 clean laps (both are named assumptions in `pca_compute.py`, `MIN_LAP_SHARE` and `MIN_CLEAN_LAPS`). The matrix has **534 rows from 30 cached races** (14 to 20 rows per race, median 18); of the 598 (driver, race) pairs with lap rows, 64 (driver, race) pairs are left out by the lap-share rule and none by the clean-lap rule (`pca_rebuild.txt`, `pca_diagnostic.txt`).

**12 features per row** (`pca_compute.py` `FEATURE_COLS`, built by `race_feature_rows`; laps, stints and stops use the shared definitions of `data/processors/stints.py` and `data/processors/degradation.py`, clean laps as in `docs/DEGRADATION_METHODOLOGY.md`):

| Feature | Description | Unit |
|---------|-------------|------|
| `pace_delta_pct` | Mean fuel-corrected clean-lap time, relative to the median of the same race's rows | % |
| `best_lap_delta_pct` | Fastest clean lap (raw time), relative to the race median | % |
| `recent_pace_delta_pct` | Mean fuel-corrected time of the last `RECENT_LAPS` = 5 clean laps (an assumption in `pca_compute.py`), relative to the race median | % |
| `sector1_delta_pct`, `sector2_delta_pct`, `sector3_delta_pct` | Mean sector time (raw) over the clean laps, relative to the race median | % |
| `consistency_pct` | Pooled residual sigma of the per-stint fits (`consistency_sigma`), as a percent of the driver's mean clean lap | % |
| `degradation_slope` | Lap-weighted mean of the per-stint tyre-age slopes (fuel-corrected, `stint_fits`) | s/lap |
| `pit_count` | Pit stops (tyre changes, decision R2): stints visible at the clock minus one | count |
| `avg_stint_length`, `max_stint_length` | Completed laps of the visible stints | laps |
| `grid_position` | Starting position (a pit-lane start counts as the back of the grid) | rank |

"Relative to the race median" is `100 * (s / median of s over the race's eligible rows - 1)`. Pace and sector features are relative to the field of the same race, so the circuit (lap length, weather) does not enter the PCA. Not features (the comment under `FEATURE_COLS`): the stint count (equal to stops + 1), the track and air temperatures (constant within a race: pure race identity), absolute lap and sector times (circuit length), and the outcome (finishing position, position change, gap to the leader). The finishing position is kept only as point metadata, for the colour and the tooltip of post-race rows.

**Fit** (`fit_pca`): missing values are filled with the column medians of the fitted rows, then every feature is standardized (scikit-learn `StandardScaler`, zero mean, unit variance) and `PCA(n_components=2)` is fitted. A component's sign is arbitrary, so it is flipped to have its largest loading positive (or, for a fit without the race on screen, to agree with the full fit's component). Each axis is labelled with the two features of largest absolute loading (`_axis_label`): PC1 "Race Pace vs Field & Sector 2 vs Field", PC2 "Avg Stint Length & Pit Stops".

**What the components capture** (`pca_rebuild.txt`, `pca_diagnostic.txt`): PC1 44.1% and PC2 20.3% of the variance (64.5% together). PC1 is the pace axis (the six pace and sector features and the grid position load 0.335 to 0.424 on it) and is nearly free of race identity (eta-squared of the race 0.028); PC2 is the stint axis (average stint length 0.602, pit stops -0.562, longest stint 0.519), and the race explains 0.558 of its variance, since stop counts and stint lengths depend on the circuit. Eta-squared of the race is the between-race share of a component's sum of squares (`eta_squared`; 1 = constant within every race, 0 = every race has the same mean). Every component (`pca_diagnostic.txt`; the biplot draws PC1 and PC2):

Two feature pairs are correlated at |r| >= 0.9 (pace with sector 2, r = 0.918; pace with sector 3, r = 0.905); 168 of the 534 degradation slopes (31.5%) are negative, median 0.0298 s/lap (`pca_diagnostic.txt`).

## 2. Why PCA and not t-SNE

1. **Interpretability via loadings.** PCA produces eigenvectors that we draw as biplot arrows; the strategist can read "PC1 is pace, PC2 is stints and stops" directly from the arrows. t-SNE has no such mapping: its axes are meaningless, and there are no loadings to display.

2. **Deterministic, comparable axes.** PCA is deterministic: the same rows always give the same cross-race biplot. The live map is refitted every lap on the current means, SDs and eigenvectors of the field, so each axis label is the share of the variance that axis captures at that lap and the crosshair is the current field mean. To keep the map continuous from lap to lap, the new top-two eigenvectors are turned inside their own plane onto the previous lap's axes (orthogonal Procrustes, `livePCA.js` `LIVE_PCA_ALIGN = 'rotation'`); points and loading arrows use the same turned basis, and the map draws one arrow per input in the fit (`ARROW_COUNT`), so every dot sits at the field mean plus its standardized features times the arrows drawn. On every lap of the 30 cached races (1720 laps) the dots drawn match that sum to within 6.02e-14 PC units and their mean is the crosshair (`pca_repair_check.txt`, section 2; with the arrows the map draws, 0 laps leave an input without its arrow, `live_pca_rewind.txt`, section 4). During a replay the previous lap's axes are those a replay from the first lap draws (`raceSituationReference.js` `replayReferenceAt`: every lap fitted at its lap clock and turned onto the lap before), never whatever was computed last, so a lap reached by playing, a lap seek or a step back is drawn on the same axes: over 1690 one-lap steps back on the 30 cached races the lap stepped to is drawn as the forward pass drew it (largest mean dot distance 0.00e+0 PC units; turned > 45 deg or reflected 0 of 1690), and a step back moves the dots as much as a step forward (median 0.56, max 1.98 PC units; `live_pca_rewind.txt`, sections 1 to 3). This holds for lap-level navigation. With replay frames, a click on the timeline in the middle of a lap refits the map at that moment (the store computes the features at the clock on screen), while playing draws the map computed at the lap's first frame for the whole lap (`computeRaceSituation` runs once per lap), so the same lap can be drawn turned or mirrored in the two cases. Live, where the clock only moves forward, the previous computation is the reference. Compared with aligning only the signs of the eigenvectors, the rotation lowers the largest lap-to-lap mean move of the points from 3.26 to 1.98 PC units (median 0.65 to 0.56); on Monza 2024 lap 42, where the two leading eigenvalues are close and the principal axes turn inside their plane, the mean move is 0.47 instead of 2.67 (`pca_repair_check.txt`, section 3). The price: the displayed axes are an orthonormal basis of the PC1-PC2 plane rather than PC1 and PC2 themselves (median angle between axis 1 and PC1 22.6 degrees; the two axes together always capture PC1 + PC2, `pca_repair_check.txt`, section 4), so the map titles them "Axis 1" and "Axis 2" and shows the plane's total. t-SNE is stochastic and would produce a different layout on every recomputation.

3. **No false negatives.** PCA preserves Euclidean distances in the projection up to the dropped components: two drivers that are far apart in 2D are also far apart in the full feature space. t-SNE models similarity as conditional probabilities on local density and amplifies separations, so two genuinely similar drivers could end up far apart in the embedding (false negative). For strategy comparison, missing a similarity is more dangerous than a spurious proximity.

4. **Euclidean distance is the right metric.** The features are continuous numeric quantities on comparable scales after standardization, and "similar strategy profile" corresponds to Euclidean proximity in this space, which PCA preserves.

There is no t-SNE projection in the code: the `/api/tsne/...` paths of `backend/api/main.py` are aliases of the PCA routes, kept for old clients.

## 3. Why not UMAP or MDS

- **MDS** minimizes a stress function iteratively, is non-deterministic across runs, and introduces both false positives and false negatives. It is slower than PCA for live updates and offers no loading vectors for interpretation.
- **UMAP** shares the non-linearity and interpretability problems of t-SNE. While it preserves more global structure than t-SNE, its axes remain uninterpretable and it is non-deterministic.
- The course syllabus covers PCA, MDS, and t-SNE. UMAP is outside the scope of the lectures. PCA is the principled choice among the three taught techniques given the linear, interpretable feature space.

## 4. Trade-offs we accept

PCA introduces **false positives**: two rows that are far apart in the 12-dimensional space can project close together in 2D if they differ mainly along PC3-PC12 (35.5% of the variance, `pca_diagnostic.txt`). The biplot mitigates this with the loading arrows, and the lasso analysis compares the selected rows feature by feature.

## 5. How PCA is integrated in the analysis flow

This is the VA requirement: dimensionality reduction integrated in the analysis pipeline, with analytics triggered by visual interaction.

1. **Data loading.** `GET /api/pca/embeddings` serves the points, the loadings, the explained variance, the eta-squared of each axis and the axis labels (store `loadPCAEmbeddings`, `apiClient.getPCAEmbeddings`). Without a race on screen it serves the post-race artefact (`load_pca_embeddings`, `backend/data/processed/pca_embeddings.json`, built from `pca_feature_matrix.csv` when missing). With the race on screen and its clock (`?year&round&session_now`, store `pcaRequestOf`), the backend fits the axes **without** that race and computes its rows from the laps completed at `session_now` only (`pca_embeddings_at`): at the end of leader lap 10 of Monza 2024 its 19 points are rows as of lap 10, with no finishing position (`pca_clock_check.txt`, section 1). While another session of the same weekend is on screen (practice, qualifying, sprint) the race has not started (`session_now` 0) and has no point. Once the race on screen reaches its end, the post-race artefact is served. In live mode the clock is the end of the latest lap row received. The static demo (no backend, `frontend/src/utils/demoMode.js`) answers this request and the one of step 3 in the browser with a port of the same module (`frontend/src/utils/pcaSelectionStats.js`, the bases exported by `backend/scripts/export_demo_data.py`; parity test `frontend/src/__tests__/demo.pcaSelectionStats.test.js`).

2. **Biplot rendering.** `PCABiplot.jsx` draws the points coloured by team or by finishing class (podium, points, no points), with the loading arrows. The race on screen is drawn with stars; the shape legend says what they are taken at ("at lap N" during a replay, "whole race" after it). Its result and its post-race rows are never drawn before that race has ended at the clock on screen. Filters: all races, the season, the race on screen.

3. **Lasso selection (visual interaction).** Shift+drag draws a lasso, a click selects one point; the selected point ids go to the store (`setSelectedPCAPoints` -> `selectedPCAPoints`), the drivers of those rows become the driver selection (`setSelectedDrivers`), and `fetchPCAAnalysis` sends them to `POST /api/pca/analyze-selection` (the former `/api/tsne/analyze-selection` is kept as an alias), with the same clock as the biplot.

4. **Backend analytics** (`analyze_selection`), labelled "Descriptive differences, not a test" (`ANALYSIS_LABEL`):
   - per feature, **Hedges' g** of the selection against the other rows (small-sample corrected), its 95% **normal-approximation interval** g +/- 1.96 SE with SE^2 = (n1 + n2) / (n1 n2) + g^2 / (2 (n1 + n2)), the large-sample standard error of a standardized mean difference (Hedges and Olkin 1985, *Statistical Methods for Meta-Analysis*; `pca_compute.py` `g_interval`), and N of both groups. A group of one row (a clicked point) gets no interval (`MIN_GROUP_FOR_INTERVAL = 2`): it has no spread of its own. On random selections, where the true difference is 0, the interval excludes 0 in at most 6.4% of the cases at every tested size from 2 to 50 rows, while the percentile bootstrap used before excluded 0 in 92.7% of the single-row cases and in 16.3% at 5 rows (`pca_interval_coverage.txt`): resampling one or two rows gives one or a few distinct values, so a bootstrap is degenerate exactly for the small selections a click or a short lasso makes;
   - a **random-selection baseline** (`random_selection_baseline`, `BASELINE_DRAWS` = 1000 draws, seeded with `SELECTION_SEED`): the |g| that random selections of the same size reach, and the threshold a feature must pass to be marked "above random" (95th percentile of the largest |g| over all features in those random selections). The selection was drawn on a projection of these same features, so large |g| values are expected by construction: of 200 random 5-row selections, 41.0% show at least one |g| >= 0.8, while 3.0% pass the random threshold (1.399 for 5 rows; `pca_selection_baseline.txt`);
   - during a replay the baseline is **stratified by row kind**: the rows of the race on screen are taken at the replay clock and the other races are whole races, so each random selection draws as many rows of each kind as the selection holds, from the rows of that kind, and the statistics block states the composition and the lap ("selection: 3 rows of the race on screen at lap 20, 2 rows of finished races; baseline drawn with the same mix"; a selection of finished rows only says that the other rows include the race on screen at that lap). A selection of rows of the race on screen only is therefore compared with random groups of that race's rows (a baseline within the race on screen), while after the race the same selection is compared with random rows of every race. Drawing from all rows, as before, flagged a selection of rows of the race in progress by the mix alone: over 15 replay clocks of 5 races, random selections of 2 to 8 of its rows flagged a progress-dependent feature in 63.5% of the cases with the former baseline and 1.7% with the stratified one (some feature: 73.0% and 5.9%), and random mixed selections flagged some feature in 30.9% and 5.4% of the cases (`pca_stratified_baseline.txt`, section 1; the first stratified version, with one threshold for all features, is the rule `round4` of `pca_mixed_threshold.txt`); without rows at a clock (post-race) the stratified baseline is the former one (section 2). A finite clock at or after the end of the race's last timed lap gives the post-race answer, since the race's rows are then its whole-race rows. A feature whose distribution moves with race progress is marked **progress-dependent** on its row while the compared rows mix the two kinds (`PROGRESS_DEPENDENT_FEATURES`): pit stops and stint lengths (at a quarter of a race the median over the races of their shift is 1.984, 1.752 and 1.879 matrix SDs away from the same race's post-race rows) and the tyre degradation slope (its spread is 2.49 times the post-race spread at a quarter of the race; `pca_progress_features.txt`). The baseline is cached per content hash of the rows compared (their values and kinds), the selection size and its composition: two clocks inside one leader lap give different keys, and the same request gets the same answer whatever was asked before (`pca_stratified_baseline.txt`, section 3);
   - with rows of the two kinds, **each feature has its own threshold** (`THRESHOLD_PER_FEATURE`; with rows of one kind, after a race or before the race on screen has a row at the clock, one threshold for all, `THRESHOLD_MAX`): in random selections with the same mix the progress-dependent features reach far larger |g| than the others by the mix alone, so the 95th percentile of the largest |g| over all features was theirs, and pace, sectors, consistency and grid could almost never pass it. Each feature is now held to the same quantile level of its own random |g| (0.992 to 0.994 at the four clocks of section 1 of the report below), that level chosen so that 5% of the random selections pass the threshold of some feature (`common_level_thresholds`: the maximum of per-feature statistics standardised by ranks, the min-p form of Westfall and Young 1993, *Resampling-Based Multiple Testing*); each row carries its threshold and the panel draws it. At lap 20 of Monza 2024, 5 rows of the race on screen with pace shifted by 1.5 matrix SDs were flagged in 0.0% of 60 selections with one threshold for all and in 55.0% with per-feature thresholds; of 96 real lassos (the 5 rows of the race on screen lowest or highest in a feature that is not progress-dependent, 6 clocks) 1 and 68 are flagged; on random selections some feature is flagged in 6.3% (rows of the race on screen only), 4.7% (mixed) and 5.6% (finished rows only) of the cases. Post-race the rule and the answers are unchanged (`pca_mixed_threshold.txt`);
   - the selection's stints, tyre strategies and pit windows (in-laps of the tyre changes). Rows of the race on screen during its replay stop at the replay clock and the answer says so (`current_race`): lassoing the 19 Monza 2024 stars at lap 10 returns one pit window (lap 5) instead of the whole race's three, and the summary carries that note (`pca_clock_check.txt`, section 2; there every row of the selection is a row of the race on screen, so the summary also says that no feature can differ more than in random selections of the same size and mix: each of them is the selection itself).

5. **Analysis panel.** `PCAAnalysisPanel.jsx` shows the label, N, a forest plot of g with its interval, the baseline band and the threshold (the "Method" section explains them), the strategies and stints (Patterns) and the pit windows (Details); clicking a pit window sets `selectedLapRange`, clicking a driver there makes it the focus driver and the only selected driver.

6. **Cross-view coordination** (`docs/COORDINATION_GRAPH.md`, sections 2 and 4, with the tables generated from the code). The lasso also selects the drivers of the selected rows (`selectedDrivers`) in every view, and `StrategyTimeline` draws the selection's tyre strategies (`pcaStrategyAnalysis`) as extra rows under the drivers. A pit window clicked in the panel sets the one lap range of the dashboard (`selectedLapRange`, equal to `brushLapRange`): `StrategyTimeline` shades those laps, each row of the Timing Sheet (`LapTimeChart`) shows the driver's latest lap inside them and its lap-range bar shows them, the Position Tracker moves its brush to them, `TireDegradation` fits them and `TelemetryStrip` in laps mode compares them. The biplot itself does not react to a lap range, and the Track Map has no lap-range view.

This chain (lasso on the biplot -> backend comparison of the selection -> cross-view highlighting) is analytics triggered by visual interaction, integrated in the analysis flow.

## 6. The live PCA (Race Situation)

- Inputs (`raceSituationFeatures.js` `FEATURE_NAMES`, 7 per driver, completed laps only at the clock): position, gap to the leader, recent pace, pace trend, tyre age, stint number, position change. Until every driver in the field has 2 clean racing laps the two pace columns are left out of the fit (constant 0) and the map says so (`paceInPca`).
- Fit (`livePCA.js` `computePCA`): standardization with the lap's field, covariance matrix, Jacobi eigen-decomposition, top two eigenvectors turned onto the reference (section 2).
- A click on a dot selects the driver, a double-click focuses it, a lasso sets the driver selection; Strategy Analysis beside it then compares the selected drivers with the rest of the field on their race-state features (these inputs plus the predicted pace and the laps to the likely stop; a standardized mean difference per feature, computed in the browser; `docs/COORDINATION_GRAPH.md`, section 5).
