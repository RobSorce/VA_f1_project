# Use cases

## 1. Purpose

This document presents five use cases of the dashboard. Each follows one member of a race team (a race strategist, a race engineer or a team data analyst) through a real decision of a cached race, step by step in the user interface, and compares what the dashboard showed at the decision clock with what happened afterwards. Every number in the use cases is printed by a tracked script into a file `docs/evidence/usecase_*.txt` and is cited with that file and its section; the scripts call the modules the views call, with the same arguments, at clocks a user can reach in the interface. All five use cases can be repeated in the static demo, https://robsorce.github.io/VA_f1_project/, which needs no backend and carries the four races they use (2024 R16 Monza, 2024 R5 China, 2024 R7 Imola and 2025 R16 Monza). Section 6 lists the scripts and how to run them.

## 2. Users and tasks

The intended user is a race-team expert ([INTENDED_USER.md](INTENDED_USER.md)). The use cases distinguish three roles of a team's race operation, which share the dashboard but not the questions. Tasks are abstracted after Brehmer and Munzner: *why* (consume: discover, present; search: lookup, locate, browse, explore; query: identify, compare, summarize) and *how* (select, navigate, filter, derive, encode), with Munzner's targets (trends, outliers, distributions, dependencies, clusters). The last column names the use case that exercises the task.

**P1, race strategist** (pit wall, during the race). Owns the stop calls and the race plan, and works under time pressure at the lap the decision is due, mostly in the Pit Wall home and the "Pit Window" layout (Track Map, Circle of Doom, Strategy Timeline).

## 4. The use cases

Conventions common to the five use cases:

- The walkthroughs run in the static demo in a window of 1536 x 864 CSS px or larger (a 1920 x 1080 display at 125 % scaling); a step that depends on the window size says so.
- Clocks are given as the lap counter ("LAP 29/53") and the elapsed replay time that the timeline player prints ("38:52 elapsed"), with the session time in seconds where the evidence needs it. A *lap seek* (→ and ←, Ctrl+K "Jump to Lap", or a lap click on the expanded timeline) lands on the first replay frame at which the leader is on that lap; at that frame most other cars are still on the previous lap. The static demo has a replay frame every 0.8 s, the local application every 0.4 s.
- A readout that changes inside a decision window is given with the share of the window during which it is on screen; the dashboard itself does not display these shares.
- "Not displayed" marks a quantity that a script computes to explain a readout but that no view shows.
- Citations have the form "(docs/evidence/usecase_<key>.txt, section X)".

### UC1. Monza 2024, race strategist: hold the one-stop or make a second stop (laps 33–36)

Script `frontend/scripts/usecases/uc1_monza24_strategy.mjs`; evidence `docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt`.

#### Persona and moment

The race strategist of the Ferrari pit wall during the 2024 Italian Grand Prix (round 16, 53 laps, no safety car, virtual safety car or red flag (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section A1)). Charles Leclerc (LEC) started on mediums, stopped at the end of lap 15 and has run hards since lap 16 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section B4). At the clock used throughout (the replay frame at which the leader, Oscar Piastri (PIA), starts lap 35, with 33 of LEC's laps completed) PIA leads on 18-lap-old hards, LEC is second, 5.6 s behind, on hards of the same age, Carlos Sainz (SAI) is third (17.5 s) and Lewis Hamilton (HAM) fourth (18.8 s) (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section B1). McLaren has just stopped Lando Norris (NOR) a second time: his in-lap was lap 32, and he runs sixth, 29.8 s behind the leader, on new hards (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section B1). The McLaren pit wall is the counterpart of this case: PIA, the leader, has not yet made the second stop that NOR has made.

The question becomes visible to the dashboard only gradually. At the lap-33 clock NOR has not entered the pits (his pit entry comes 4.5 s later (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section B2)). At the lap-34 clock his stop is in progress, and his race-state features still come from his in-lap row (third, 8.8 s), against sixth and 29.8 s once the out-lap is completed (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section B3). Laps 35 and 36 are therefore the clean decision clocks. At lap 35 three other cars (RUS, BOT, HUL) have a stop in progress, none of them near LEC's places; at lap 36 only BOT has (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section B3).

#### Task

Should LEC make a second stop, at the end of his next lap (in-lap 35, the earliest stop the Strategy Simulator accepts at the lap-35 clock, which is the Circle of Doom's "pit in 1") or two laps later (in-lap 37), or run the hards fitted on lap 16 to the flag, a 38-lap stint? This is task S4 (strategy alternative): *why* = query-compare two derived distributions of finishing position (the plan against a saved plan); *how* = navigate (seek to the decision lap), select (focus the car), derive (a paired what-if run); target = distributions. It is supported by E1 (tyre wear: query-summarize a trend, the degradation slope over a selected lap range; how = select a stint, which sets the lap range) and by S5 (query-summarize the projected finishing distribution of the field). The McLaren side asks the same S4 question about PIA's second stop.

#### Walkthrough

1. Open the demo, choose Season 2024, Grand Prix "R16 — Italian Grand Prix", Session RACE, and press ENTER SESSION. The replay opens on the Pit Wall, paused at lap 1. Keep it paused for the whole walkthrough (Space toggles play).
2. Seek to the decision lap: Ctrl+K, "Jump to Lap", "Lap 35". This lands on the first frame at which the leader is on lap 35 (demo frame 3609, session time 6237.694 s). The lap-mode clock, the full application's frame and this frame give the same knowledge state, features and Monte Carlo seed, so the numbers are the same in the demo and in the full application (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section A2). The right-arrow key and a lap click on the expanded timeline land on the same frame. While the replay is paused every readout below holds. If it plays on, the first lap or pit event of another car arrives after 3.2 s (4 demo frames, 3.8 % of the 83.2 s lap), and LEC completes his lap 34 after 5.2 s (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section A3). Coming from lap 1 also means that the Race Prediction panel has no earlier projection to hold its labels on (step 5).
3. Open the dock button "Layouts" and choose the preset "Simulator" (Strategy Simulator, Strategy Timeline and Race Prediction).
4. Focus LEC: click his row in the Leaderboard on the left (this selects him), then press F with the pointer still on the row (this makes him the focus). Use this path, not a double-click. A double-click also focuses him, but its two clicks toggle his selection on and off, so the bar click of step 6 would then select him and hide the other drivers' stop labels; in the full application a double-click also requests the backend forecast, whose optimal pit lap Tyre Degradation can then show as a "NEXT PIT WINDOW".
5. Race Prediction, Projections tab. The header reads "Pit loss: 26.2s median, race (P10–P90 24.4–33.8s, n=20)", "Lap noise: ±0.39s race (n=549 laps)" and "SC rate: 1.1%/lap prior" (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section D1). The rows are paged (◀ ▶). PIA: P1, 94 % podium, P5–P95 range P1–P4. LEC: P2, now P2, 82 % podium, range P1–P4, expected position 2.4 in the label's tooltip; the tooltip of LEC's P1 bar reads 26.2 % of the runs, PIA's 57.6 %. SAI is P3 with 61 %, HAM P3 with 53 % and NOR P5 with 8 % (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section E1). LEC's P2 label is the same on every path to laps 35 and 36; at laps 33 and 34 it is P2 or P3 depending on the path (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section E2).
6. Strategy Timeline. Click LEC's hard bar first: this sets the lap range L16–L33 and toggles LEC off the selection (the focus stays). With no driver selected, the Strategy Timeline draws every driver's stops; while any driver is selected it draws them only for the selected drivers, so before this click only LEC's stop was drawn. LEC's bars are M L1–L15 and H L16–L33, the second one ongoing, with his stop labelled "26.9s". NOR's are M L1–L14, H L15–L32 and a new H from L33, with his stops labelled "24.7s" and "22.9s" (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section B4).
7. Tyre Degradation is not in the preset (key 2 opens a different view, Tyre Health / Pace Retention). Click "+" on the Race Prediction pane and choose "Tyre Degradation"; it opens as a second tab of that pane. With the lap range, the chart draws only LEC's hard stint: "S2 H", with the labels "-0.033 s/lap (fuel-corr.*)" and "-0.063 s/lap (raw)"; the tooltip gives the fit's 16 clean laps. The banner reads "BRUSHED LAPS L16 – L33 · fitted L16 – L33 · raw −63 ms/lap · fuel-corr.* −33 ms/lap", and no "NEXT PIT WINDOW" line appears (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section C1). The reason (a fitted degradation that is not positive) is in the fit's response, but no view displays it, nor the slope's standard error (0.007 s/lap) (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section C1). Focusing PIA and clicking his hard bar (L17–L34, 17 laps), or focusing NOR and clicking his completed hard bar (L15–L32, 14 laps), gives -0.034 and -0.035 s/lap (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section C2); with the focus alone the range stays L16–L33. After such a comparison, focus LEC again (click + F on his row) and click his hard bar before step 8: with no plan yet, the Simulator takes the focus as its driver.
8. Strategy Simulator. Click "Copy Actual" (the plan becomes M15-H38: the ongoing stint extended to the flag), then "Run Simulation (1,000 sims)". Against the model's own stops this gives "• 0.0 ±0.00 NO CLEAR CHANGE" (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section F1, currentLap 35). Set S2 to 20L and S3 to H, 18L (the stint rows are paged (◀ ▶); ▶ reaches S2 and S3). The line under the editor reads "From L35: in-lap 35 → HARD · 1 earlier stop = race history" (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section F0). Click Run. #### Outcome

LEC did not stop again (M L1–15, H L16–53) and won. PIA (stops at the end of laps 16 and 38) finished 2.664 s behind, NOR (in-laps 14 and 32) 6.153 s behind, and SAI, on one stop, 15.621 s behind (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section J1). LEC led from lap 39 to the flag (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section J3). PIA rejoined third, 18.5 s behind, at the end of lap 39 and closed to 2.664 s at the flag; over the 14 laps 40–53 that both ran clean he was 1.134 s per lap faster than LEC (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section J4). NOR rose from sixth (29.8 s) after his stop to third (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, sections J1, J2).

#### What the dashboard contributed

At one reachable clock, four coordinated views answered the strategist's question, each with its own uncertainty. The Tyre Degradation fit of the selected stint showed no measurable tyre loss under the assumed fuel correction (a negative fuel-corrected slope over 16 clean laps), and therefore no pit window. Race Prediction showed where the field was heading, with its P5–P95 ranges and podium shares. The Strategy Simulator compared the second stop with the saved one-stop plan on common random numbers, so that a plan compared with itself differs by exactly 0 and the ± is the Monte Carlo error of the paired runs. The Circle of Doom translated the cost of a stop into places: from P2 to P6 at the rejoin (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section D2). The verdict did not depend on the exact lap or compound of the second stop (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section F1), and it agreed with the choice Ferrari made; LEC won. The same Simulator, asked the McLaren question, said that PIA's second stop would cost about two places and the lead (P4 against P1) (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section G1). McLaren stopped him, and he finished second, not fourth. His actual result lay inside the plan's distribution (32.7 % of the runs finish P1–P2 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section G1)) but well away from its median. Likewise LEC's win lay inside the one-stop distribution (P1 in 26.2 % of the runs at lap 35 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section F1)). The dashboard did not show the error of its own lap model, the slope's standard error, or why no pit window was proposed. Before NOR's pit entry it could not know that McLaren had stopped him (step 2).

The counterpart qualifies the Simulator's agreement with Ferrari's choice. Against a car that keeps an older set, the lap model gives a new set of hards a gain per lap equal to the compound's degradation rate times the age gap, plus a fresh-tyre delta that was 0.000 s at these clocks (the race's own estimate, clamped to 0 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section D1)). Measured afterwards on clean laps as a difference in differences against LEC, NOR's new hards gained 1.062 s per lap where the lap model gave 0.408 (ratio 0.38), and PIA's gained 1.104 s per lap where it gave 0.361 (ratio 0.33) (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section H1). The Simulator therefore credited a second stop with roughly a third of the advantage fresh hards actually had at this race. It was biased toward the one-stop, which is consistent with its pessimism about PIA (projected P4, finished P2). For LEC the bias shrinks the margin but does not obviously reverse it. A rough time balance in hindsight, not a dashboard readout, sets the stop's whole cost (the header's pit loss, 26.2 s) against the gain over the 17 laps left on new hards. That gain is 4.8 s with the lap model and 18.1–18.8 s at the measured 1.06–1.10 s per lap, a net loss of 21.4 s and of 7.4–8.1 s respectively (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section H2). The direction of the verdict survives the correction; its size (−1.7 places, ±0.21) does not include this model error. The result of a second LEC stop was never observed.

#### Limitations and uncertainty

- *Model error is not in the ±.* The interval of the verdict is the Monte Carlo error of 1,000 paired runs (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section F1). The fresh-hard gain was underestimated by a factor of about three at this race (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section H1). The measured gain is a difference in differences against a single car (LEC) and contains that car's own pace management, so the ratio is indicative.
- *In-sample priors.* The lap model's HARD rate at lap 35 (+0.014 s/lap) combines 16 hard fits of this race (6 of them negative, plain median +0.026) with the cached races' mean (+0.0174). That mean is fitted on all 30 cached races, this race included. The SC rate of 1.1 % per lap, labelled "prior" because this race had no neutralisation, is the cached races' one SC or VSC episode per 60.6 leader laps, fitted on the same 30 races with this one included, combined with this race's 34 leader laps without one (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section D1, currentLap 35).
- *Fuel correction.* The fuel-corrected slopes are the raw slopes plus an assumed constant of 0.030 s/lap (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section C). The data cannot separate fuel burn, track evolution and tyre management, so a negative slope means only that no tyre loss is measurable under that assumption, not that the tyres improved. The chart's per-stint fit and the lap model's rate are different estimates by design: 16 clean laps of one stint, against a combination of the race's fits of at least 15 laps with the prior (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, sections C1, D1).
- *Path dependence.* Race Prediction holds its P labels within the Monte Carlo error, so a label depends on the projection held before. LEC's label is P2 at laps 35–36 on every path, but P2 or P3 at laps 33–34 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section E2). The shares, ranges and expected positions do not depend on the path (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section E1).
- *Stops in progress.* A car in the pits or on its out-lap is projected from its in-lap row, and the stop under way is not charged. At lap 34 this puts NOR third (54 % podium), against 8 % at lap 35 once his out-lap is known (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, sections B3, E1). Laps 35 and 36 avoid this for the cars that matter (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section B3).
- *Clock.* All numbers hold at the paused lap-seek frame. While the replay plays, the open Race Prediction panel recomputes within the lap at each new lap or pit event of any car, the first one 0.8 to 5.6 s after the frame at laps 33–38 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section A3). A Run later in the lap takes the clock and seed of the frame it is run at. Over every clock within the lap at which the knowledge state changes, the second stop gives −1.7 to −2.1 places at lap 35 (18 clocks, podium −43 to −52 points) and −1.7 to −2.3 at lap 36 (23 clocks, podium −46 to −57 points; −2.3 at 79.2 s and 83.2 s into the lap, and the plan's card P5 rather than P4 at most clocks after 17.6 s) (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section I1).
- *Simulator mechanics.* Planned stops on laps before the leader's current lap are dropped, so after a lap step the plan has to be edited (step 9; docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section F2). At a lap-seek frame every car but the leader is still on the previous lap, so a stop at the end of the lap in progress (the Circle of Doom's "Pit now", in-lap 34 at the lap-35 clock) cannot be planned in the Simulator. The saved comparisons do not record the driver: "vs" applies a saved plan to whichever driver the current plan is for, hence the Clear in step 11.
- *What is not displayed.* The slope's standard error, the reason for "no pit window" and the lap model's fresh-hard gain are shown by no view. The degradation rate of the lap model appears only in the Circle of Doom tooltip (step 10).
- *Full application.* A double-click focus requests the backend forecast, whose optimal pit lap Tyre Degradation shows as a pit window when the selected fit has none; use click + F there (step 4). The demo's lap rows, track status and results are identical to the local cache (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section A1).

### UC2. Monza 2024, team data analyst: post-race debrief of the strategy split

Script `frontend/scripts/usecases/uc2_monza24_analyst.mjs`, with its backend half `backend/scripts/usecase_monza24_analyst.py`; evidence `docs/evidence/usecase_uc2_monza24_analyst.txt`.

#### Persona and moment

A team data analyst reviews the 2024 Italian Grand Prix (2024 R16) after the race, with the replay at its end. The race ran 53 laps with 20 drivers and had no safety-car or virtual-safety-car period (docs/evidence/usecase_uc2_monza24_analyst.txt, header). The field split between two strategy classes: of the 19 drivers that have a row in the cross-race projection, 9 stopped once and 10 stopped twice or more; the 20th, TSU, retired without a stop and has no row (docs/evidence/usecase_uc2_monza24_analyst.txt, section 2). #### Task

Did stopping two or more times give the cars that did it anything that the one-stop did not, and what do the two groups look like in compounds and pit windows? A second question follows from the first: how much can be read into a comparison of one or two cars? This is task A2 (cross-race strategy patterns), applied to one race, with part of A5 (how the views show uncertainty). *Why*: search-explore (find groups in the projection), then query-compare (each group against all other rows) and query-summarize (strategies, pit windows); targets: clusters, attribute differences and their distributions. *How*: navigate (to the end of the replay), filter (the race on screen), encode (colour by finishing class), select (lasso and click), and derive (Hedges' g with an interval, against a random-selection baseline, computed on request).

The biplot made the strategy split visible without the analyst first knowing who stopped when: the two classes lie on opposite sides of PC2, one lasso per class catches it, and the Finish colour shows a podium car in each class, which already argues against reading the result as a property of the strategy class. #### Limitations and uncertainty

- *Archive.* The PCA basis and the comparison rows are all 30 cached races, this race included, and among them the 14 run after Monza 2024: 2024 R17–R24 and the 2025 rounds, Monza 2025 among them (docs/evidence/usecase_uc2_monza24_analyst.txt, section 6). The geometry holds: PC2 is then labelled "Avg Stint Length & Longest Stint" (21.8 %), and the groups still separate (PC2 +0.93 to +1.77 against −2.64 to −0.81, no other star inside either hull). The separation on PC2 and the best-lap flag hold in every archive; the one-stoppers' stop-count flag sits on its threshold (g −1.00 against 1.028 today), so today's "Mixed cluster" for them depends on races run later.
- *Selection on the projection.* Both groups were drawn on PC2, which is built from stint length and stop count, so large |g| on those features is expected by construction, as the panel's Method note says; the stint flags of the two-or-more-stop group restate its strategy. - *Clock.* The post-race readout exists on the last replay frame only (docs/evidence/usecase_uc2_monza24_analyst.txt, section 1). At any earlier clock, the `End` key's included, the race's rows are taken at the clock, the axes are fitted without the race and each feature has its own threshold, so the readings differ (docs/evidence/usecase_uc2_monza24_analyst.txt, section 1).
- *What is displayed.* The twelve features are post-race summaries computed by the backend's definitions; the finishing position is not among them. Coordinates are not displayed, only positions. The Patterns strip draws the stint lengths of one row of each pattern, and when two patterns are equally common the one listed first, and named "most common" in the summary, depends on the order of the selection: the lasso of all 19 stars holds 7 MEDIUM-HARD and 7 MEDIUM-HARD-HARD rows, and the summary names MEDIUM -> HARD at 37 % (docs/evidence/usecase_uc2_monza24_analyst.txt, section 3; the Patterns of the two group lassos are not tied).
- *Demo and local application.* The demo answers in the browser with a port of the backend's analysis. For the six selections of this case it gives the same numbers and texts as the local backend (largest difference 0) and the same biplot coordinates (docs/evidence/usecase_uc2_monza24_analyst.txt, section 5).

#### Task

Can LEC stop now without rejoining in the midfield train, and if not, how many more laps of gap does he need? The engineer's part is to know whether the car will come out in clean air and to tell the driver whether to keep building the gap. This joins task S1 of the strategist (pit now or in k laps) with task E5 of the engineer (traffic at the rejoin). *Why*: query-identify a derived attribute of one item (LEC's projected rejoin place, with its distribution and a traffic flag), query-compare alternatives (pitting now against pitting in k laps), and search-locate the items that satisfy a threshold (the cars behind by less than one pit loss, the "Gain the place" list); the target is a trend (the gap to the cars behind growing past the pit loss) and the dependency of the rejoin place on it. *How*: select the focus car, navigate in time (lap seek, playback), and the stepper for the derived alternatives; the view derives the pit window, the rejoin probabilities, the traffic flag and the "pit in k laps" table.

#### Limitations and uncertainty

- *Readouts change as cars cross the line and enter the pits.* The "P4 → P11" readout after LEC's lap 27 is on screen for 5.5 s, and two more readouts follow it before his next line crossing (docs/evidence/usecase_uc3_monza25_cod.txt, sections 3, 4a, 4e). The shares above, not single instants, describe what the engineer saw. Every readout lasts at least one demo frame inside W, and at all 2338 frame clocks checked, backend and demo, the memo key, and with it every readout, is that of its event interval (docs/evidence/usecase_uc3_monza25_cod.txt, section 3).
- *What the probabilities carry.* Only the uncertainty of the pit loss (1000 draws, a bootstrap of the 5 to 8 stops measured so far), not the stops of other cars that have not started, nor pace changes. A rival's stop enters the window at its pit entry, modelled from its in-lap row; before that the car is projected at its recent pace. The rejoin rank uses each car's recent pace held constant, and the traffic flag is the assumption that a crossing less than 1.0 s behind another car means traffic (docs/evidence/usecase_uc3_monza25_cod.txt, sections 2, 3). The documented backtest gives the scale of the error: over 709 green stops the window's place was exact in 46.7 % of the stops and within one place in 72.8 %, 87.3 % exact on the 110 stops in which no rival stopped before the rejoin; on the 18 green stops of this race, 77.8 % exact (docs/evidence/usecase_uc3_monza25_cod.txt, section 9, quoting docs/evidence/cod_backtest.txt). One stop of one race is an illustration, not a validation.
- *The lap model.* The "pit in k laps" comparison rests on the race simulation's lap model. Its degradation per compound combines this race's stint fits with the mean of the cached races, a prior fitted on all 30 cached races, this race included (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section D1, which prints the prior's provenance and quotes the list of cached races, 2025 R16 among them), so it is in-sample here. In W the MEDIUM estimate was negative (−0.020 to −0.017 s/lap while the in-lap-33 row is displayed) and the fresh-tyre delta came from one stint (+0.000 s) until 39:56 elapsed (docs/evidence/usecase_uc3_monza25_cod.txt, sections 4a, 5); with a negative fit the model makes a new set slower, which is what produced "+3 vs now; waiting gains 2.9 s" at LAP 29. The view's help text states this limitation. The rows also assume no traffic and, unless "rivals" is pressed, no stops by the other cars; pressing "rivals" did not change the in-lap-33 row in W (docs/evidence/usecase_uc3_monza25_cod.txt, section 5).
- *Rounding.* The gap labels are rounded to 0.1 s and can tie with the pit loss in the centre (OCO +25.1 s against 25.1 s); the threshold lists, not the rounded numbers, say which side a car is on (docs/evidence/usecase_uc3_monza25_cod.txt, sections 3, 6).
- *Track status.* The window was green throughout, so no safety-car caveat applies; under a neutralisation the view would keep green-flag figures (docs/evidence/usecase_uc3_monza25_cod.txt, section 1).
- *Not displayed by any view:* the predicted crossing times, the ex post gaps of the counterfactual, the flat-model rows and the gaps computed from the lap rows; the gap labels display the latter rounded, once both cars have completed the lap (docs/evidence/usecase_uc3_monza25_cod.txt, sections 6, 7, 8).
- *A label resting on one stop.* A secondary vignette from the same race shows what a probability label cannot say. At LAP 18 (23:40 elapsed), with BEA (Haas) as focus, the centre reads "pit loss 24.7 s (1 stop)" and "BEA pits now: P11 → P19", the label "P19 100%"; the tooltip gives "Pit loss 24.7 s (p10–p90 24.7–24.7 s, 1 stops)" and "Draws 1000, bootstrap of 1 stops", and the hatched p10–p90 arc has no width (docs/evidence/usecase_uc3_monza25_cod.txt, section 10). From the first measured stop (LAW, lap 9) until BEA's own stop was measured, every window rested on one stop for 757.8 s of race time (docs/evidence/usecase_uc3_monza25_cod.txt, section 10). From the LAP 18 seek to BEA's pit entry on lap 18 the readout stayed "P19 100%" on one stop, and he rejoined P19 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc3_monza25_cod.txt, section 10); at the seek clock BEA has not yet completed lap 17, so the first part of that interval models a stop one lap earlier than the one he made, and a single stop is an illustration, not a check. The "100%" looks the same as LEC's "P6 100%" at LAP 34, which rests on eight stops with a p10–p90 of 22.9–26.0 s (docs/evidence/usecase_uc3_monza25_cod.txt, section 2). The label alone does not tell them apart; the stop count in the centre ("(1 stop)" against "(8 stops)"), the tooltip's p10–p90 and the width of the hatched arc do. To reach it: Ctrl+K, "Jump to Lap", "Lap 18", then click BEA's car on the ring (the focus moves to BEA).

#### Task

Three questions with the same structure: under a neutralisation, is a stop now cheaper, and where does the car rejoin? For NOR (VSC): box at the end of this lap, and in which place does he come out? For VER (SC): box now, and does he keep the lead? For SAI (SC): stay out, and which of the cars ahead fall behind him if they stop? This is strategist task S3, with S1 and S2. *Why*: the strategist first consumes to discover an event (a neutralisation has started), then queries to identify a derived attribute of one item (the projected rejoin place of the focus car and its distribution) and to compare alternatives (pit now against staying out); for SAI the task also searches to locate the cars that satisfy a threshold (ahead by less than one pit loss: the "Within reach" list). *How*: navigate to the event (lap seek, play and pause), select a focus car; the view derives the pit window and the rejoin probabilities. Targets: a single item's rank and its distribution, and the dependency of that rank on the pit loss.

#### Limitations and uncertainty

- *Green-flag figures under neutralisation.* The help of the Circle of Doom states: "Under Safety Car or VSC the pit loss, the paces and the reference lap stay green-flag figures (no validated SC / VSC estimator yet)" (`frontend/src/utils/chartHelp.js`, circle limitations), and the centre shows the note at every neutralised clock (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, sections 2a, 3c, 4a). The two stops that were made completed the projected lap much later than projected (NOR +60.2 s from the lap-23 clock, VER +81.0 s from the first SC frame; docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, sections 2d, 3c); what matters for the rank is that the error differs between the car that stops and the cars that do not, which a green-flag pit loss cannot capture.
- *Readouts that swing as cars cross the line.* During the VSC the rank compares cars that have already completed a slow neutralised lap (their projection starts from that late line) with cars still projected at green pace from an earlier line (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, sections 2d, 3a). The headline therefore changes as other cars complete such laps: NOR's from P5 to P2 within one lap; VER's to P7 for 46 frames (36.8 s) and back to P3 by 38:37 elapsed (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, sections 2c, 3b). These swings are an artefact of the green-flag assumption, not new information about the race; the help states the assumption but not this consequence. NOR's last readout shows it: VER, who had just completed a 134.3 s lap, was projected 1.3 s behind NOR at the end of lap 23 and crossed 21.0 s ahead of him, so P2 at 79 % became P3 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, sections 2d, 5d). By the SC deployment the cars around VER and SAI had all completed lap 22 under the VSC, and the readouts of VER and SAI stayed put (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, sections 3b, 3c, 4a, 4b).
- *What the probabilities carry.* Only the pit-loss uncertainty: a bootstrap of the 18 green-flag stops of this race on in-laps 8 to 17 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, section 6a). One of those stops lost 40.5 s (GAS, in-lap 11), 1 of 18 samples, consistent with the 6 % far entries of the labels (P10 6%, P7 6%, P12 6%) (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, sections 6a, 2c, 3b). Unseen rival stops are not in the draws: the window assumes the other cars do not stop before the focus car completes its out-lap (help text). VER's miss at the SC combines both effects: NOR, who did not stop again, was projected 9.0 s ahead of him at the end of lap 24 and crossed 1.3 s behind (green-flag pit loss and pace), and PER, projected 3.4 s ahead, stopped under the SC after the clock and crossed 5.0 s behind (an unseen rival stop) (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, sections 3c, 5b). A rival whose stop is in progress (LEC, on his out-lap at the lap-23 clock) is charged the part of the median loss that its in-lap row has not paid (+13.2 s, not displayed; docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, section 2d).
- *The "Within reach" list* uses green-flag gaps, the green-flag pit loss and equal paces. That it matched the outcome under the SC (3 of 4) is partly because the SC also bunched the field; it is consistent with the outcome, not a validation of the threshold under SC. The counterfactual (SAI pitting) is not observable (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, section 4c).
- *The readout at the VSC deployment* (P2 → P5) is the window for a stop at the end of lap 21, which NOR did not make; the decision he faced came during lap 22 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, sections 2a, 5d).
- *Timeline markers.* At the lap of each neutralisation's start the pit-stop marker of the same lap is drawn over the track-status marker, at any window width: V is covered from the first pit exit under the VSC on, and SC from its first frame, because NOR's pit exit precedes it (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, section 5a). This is a defect of the Timeline Player, recorded here and not fixed; the evidence file still describes the first VSC and SC frames as the frames a click on V and SC seeks to (sections 1d, 2a, 3c, 4a), and the walkthrough reaches them by play and pause.
- *Clocks.* The lap seeks land between 0.059 and 0.776 s after the leader's line (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, section 1c); the other readouts need play and pause, and the evidence gives the demo frames during which each holds (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, sections 2c, 3b, 4b). During play the replay clock runs between frames, so a readout reached by play and pause changes at the event itself, and the frame counts are within one frame of that. Short readouts carry their time share: NOR's readout at the first VSC frame holds 6 frames (4.8 s, 3 % of the frames from the VSC to his pit entry; section 2c), VER's "P1 → P7" 46 frames (36.8 s, 27 % of the frames from his lap-22 line to his pit entry; section 3b). On the backend's own frames (0.4 s apart) the same sequence of readouts appears, with lengths that agree to within one demo frame, and the demo session equals the backend session on the lap-row fields the script compares and on the track status (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, section 7).
- *Not displayed:* the projected crossing times and the gaps behind the rank and the lists (docs/evidence/usecase_uc4_china24_sc.txt, sections 2a, 3c, 4c, the lines marked "not displayed"). No cross-race prior enters these readouts: the Circle of Doom uses this race's own stops and laps only (`frontend/src/utils/circleOfDoom.js`).

### UC5. Imola 2024, race strategist: an undercut fight (PIA against SAI)

Script `frontend/scripts/usecases/uc5_imola24_undercut.mjs`; evidence `docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt`.

#### Persona and moment

The race strategist on the McLaren pit wall, with Oscar Piastri (PIA), at the 2024 Emilia Romagna Grand Prix (Imola, 63 laps). No lap of the race ran under a safety car, a virtual safety car or a red flag (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, section A). At the end of lap 22 PIA crosses the line in P5, 0.90 s behind Carlos Sainz (SAI, Ferrari, P4); both cars are on the MEDIUM set they started on, 22 laps old (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, sections A, D.1). By the lap-24 seek NOR (P3), the car ahead of SAI, is on a new HARD set; he was still on his MEDIUM set when PIA crossed the line at the end of lap 22 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, sections B.1, B.2). Seven green-flag stops of this race have been measured by the time the leader starts lap 24 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, sections B.1, E). The decision is due on lap 23: PIA reaches the pit entry 81.9 s after crossing the line at the end of lap 22 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, section B). #### Task

D1 (McLaren): box PIA at the end of this lap to undercut SAI, or stay out? D2 (Ferrari): PIA has boxed; cover now, or stay out and try to overcut? These are the strategist's tasks S2 (undercut or overcut against a named rival) and S1 (pit now or stay out k laps). *Why*: query-compare two items (PIA and SAI: their gap at the line and the age of their sets), search-locate the cars within one pit loss of the focus (the threshold lists "Gain the place" and "Within reach"), query-identify the projected rejoin place of one item with its distribution, and query-compare the alternatives k = 0..10 laps of waiting. *How*: select (the focus, by a click on the car; a transient hover of the rival for its gap), navigate (a lap seek, play and pause), and change two parameters of the view (the "pit in k" stepper and the "rivals" toggle).

#### Outcome

PIA boxed at the end of lap 23 (MEDIUM to HARD) and was P8 at the end of his out-lap, the place the Circle of Doom had projected, and P7 one lap later, the "pitting now instead" place of k = 1. SAI stayed out until lap 27 (MEDIUM to HARD), running P1 on laps 25 to 27, and rejoined P6 at the end of lap 28, 4.86 s behind PIA (P4); the k = 3 projection had placed SAI one position lower, P7. The two stops cost 27.5 s (PIA) and 28.2 s (SAI) against a race median of 28.0 s over 25 green-flag stops. PIA finished 4th and SAI 5th, 8.19 s apart at the end of lap 63 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, sections D.1, D.2).

#### What the dashboard contributed

For the McLaren call the Circle of Doom answered the parts of the undercut question that do not depend on the rival's reply. The stop rejoined P8 in every one of the 1000 draws of the pit loss, with STR 1.5–1.9 s ahead and never flagged as traffic at the point projection, though the tooltip's traffic probability read 16 % (31 % for the first 29.1 % of the window, demo frames) (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, sections B.1, B.3). SAI was within one pit loss ahead ("Within reach"), and each lap of waiting cost the lap model about 1.9 s against stopping now (3.5 to 3.7 s for two laps, 3.7 s for 14.6 % of the window, from the lap-24 seek; docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, section B.3). The rejoin projection matched the lap rows at both laps it covers (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, section D.2). For the Ferrari call the same view showed that a cover stop would rejoin P6 to P8, most likely P7, into traffic behind STR, and that waiting three laps cost 5.4 to 5.6 s against covering at once (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, section C.3); SAI waited, and at the end of lap 28 he was 4.86 s behind PIA instead of 0.90 s ahead (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, section D.1). Each of these readings holds over the whole decision window or is given with its time share in steps 7 to 9; the backend's frames give the same readouts with nearly the same shares (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, sections B.3, C.3).

What no reachable view showed is the head-to-head under the strategist's hypothesis about the rival: the gap between PIA and SAI after both stops, or at the flag, if SAI covers on the next lap. The Circle of Doom ranks the focus against every car at the end of its out-lap, and its "rivals" toggle schedules each rival at its likely stop (for SAI eight or nine laps away, depending on the clock), not at a lap the strategist chooses; the Monte Carlo views (Race Prediction, Strategy Simulator) also run the other cars on the model's own stops and are not part of this walkthrough. The code base has a view for exactly this question, the Undercut Calculator (`UndercutCalculator.jsx`, `utils/undercut.js` `undercutSweep`), but it is not registered in the dock, the docked layout's tab picker, the command palette or the window presets, so no control of the dashboard opens it (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, section G).

*Analysis only, not displayed by the dashboard.* The script runs the Undercut Calculator's computation with the panel's own arguments. At the start of PIA's lap 23 it would show "✓ AHEAD AFTER BOTH STOPS" with the best pit lap L23: PIA ahead by 10.59 s at the flag with SAI's likely stop (L31), and by 5.01 s with RIVAL STOPS set to L24, the cover hypothesis; with that choice every later column falls (L24 +4.72 s, L31 −0.86 s, L38 −11.65 s). From SAI's side, with PIA's stop in progress, it would show "✗ BEHIND AFTER BOTH STOPS", −2.92 s for a cover at L24 and −4.61 s for the L27 stop SAI made. In the panel's code a chosen rival lap is not a persistent setting (`UndercutCalculator.jsx:170-174`): the L24 choice would be kept through PIA's lap 23, the panel would be unavailable while PIA is stopping, and once SAI has passed lap 24 the choice is dropped and the select returns to "likely" (at 5326.21 s); scrubbing back over laps 17 to 22 would need the choice set again at every lap. Set each time to two laps after the scrubbed lap, the verdict reads BEHIND at the end of laps 17 to 19 (−3.10, −3.45, −6.52 s) and AHEAD from lap 20 (+6.20, +4.01, +5.01 s); left on "likely" it changes from lap to lap (AHEAD at the end of laps 17 and 18, BEHIND by 1.30 s at the end of lap 19, AHEAD again from lap 20) (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, sections G.1 to G.5). The same holds for the gap after both stops: with SAI in at L27 and the horizon at the end of lap 28, the sweep's arithmetic gives +7.05 s for PIA against the real +4.86 s; no view uses that horizon (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, section G.6).

A second instance at the same race shows the same boundary at lower stakes. At the start of his lap 30 GAS (Alpine, P16) is 1.9 s behind SAR (Williams); the Circle of Doom gives "GAS pits now: P16 → P19", "P19 100.0%", pit loss 27.6 s (15 stops), with SAR within reach, on every frame of GAS's decision lap, and with "rivals" on it never schedules SAR's stop within the ten laps of the stepper. SAR covered at L31; GAS rejoined P19 as projected and was 4.53 s ahead after both stops and 1.34 s ahead at the end of lap 62 (16th against 17th, both lapped) (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, section H). The rival-specific sweep, again analysis only, gives, at its best pit lap L32 (two laps after GAS's real stop at L30), GAS ahead at the flag by 28.39 s with SAR's likely stop (L47) and by 1.31 s with the cover at L31 (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, section H). The nearness of 1.31 s to the real 1.34 s is not a measure of accuracy: the sweep's figure is for another pit lap, and SAR's stop cost 30.5 s against the 27.6 s median pit loss of the window (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, section H).

#### Limitations and uncertainty

- *What the 100.0 % carries.* The rejoin probability reflects only the spread of this race's measured pit losses (7 stops at the D1 lap seek, p10–p90 26.6–28.5 s); it does not include errors in the paces, which the window holds constant at each car's recent clean laps, nor traffic beyond the 1.0 s flag.
- *In-sample lap model.* The "waiting costs" seconds and the k places come from the lap model of the race simulation; its MEDIUM and HARD degradation estimates are this race's per-stint fits combined with a cross-race prior that is fitted on all 30 cached races, Imola 2024 included, so they are in-sample with respect to this race. The future lap rows of the race are not used: rewriting every row, pit time and track-status event after the clock leaves every readout identical, while changing the focus car's past laps changes them (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, sections E, F). The fresh-tyre delta itself moved from −0.145 s (4 stints) to −0.281 s (5 stints) within the decision lap (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, sections B.1, B.2, E). The k projection compares places at the end of each out-lap, not at the flag.
- *A stop in progress.* At the D2 clock PIA's stop is modelled from its in-lap row, charged the median pit loss and projected at its pace on the old set (81.869 s per lap); in that rank, which the view does not display, SAI would come out 0.68 s ahead of PIA at the end of lap 25, whereas PIA's first flying lap on the new set took 79.907 s (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, sections C.1, D.1). At the same clock the focus card's rival gap compares each car's latest completed lap, so with PIA's in-lap unfinished it reads "+1.1s" for PIA against the ring's "+0.9" (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, section C.1).
- *Readouts within the window.* Several readouts change as other cars cross the line; steps 7 to 9 give their time shares. The analysis of the Undercut Calculator above is not displayed by the dashboard.
- *Scope.* The race ran entirely under green flag, so the Circle of Doom's green-flag pit loss and paces apply as measured. The comparison with the outcome is one race and two decisions; the counterfactual (SAI covering at L24) is not observed.

## 5. Cross-cutting observations

### 5.1 What the coordinated views and the causal replay add

*Coordinated views.* In UC1 one focus and one lap range carried the strategist's question across four views: a click on a stint of the Strategy Timeline set the lap range that Tyre Degradation fitted, the Strategy Simulator's plan was drawn over the same Strategy Timeline, Race Prediction gave the field's distribution at the same clock, and the Circle of Doom tooltip translated the stop into places. Each view answered one part with its own uncertainty, and the Simulator's verdict could be read against the slope of the stint and the projected field without changing the clock. In UC2 a lasso on the biplot drove the Cross-Race Analysis panel, and a click on a pit window there set the lap range that the Strategy Timeline shades. In UC3 to UC5 a single view, the Circle of Doom, joined what the other views show apart: the track status, the pit loss with its spread and its number of stops, the projected place with its probabilities, the traffic flag, the threshold lists and the gap tags, with the focus shared with the Leaderboard, the Timing Tower and the focus cards (UC5 read the rival's gap and tyre age in the Rivals row, UC4 the stop labels in the Strategy Timeline).

*Causal replay.* Every view reads only what had happened by the dashboard's clock, so each use case could be replayed at its decision clock and compared, in its Outcome, with the lap rows after it. The scripts check this property where it matters: rewriting the future of the race leaves the UC5 readouts identical, while a change to the focus car's past laps changes them (docs/evidence/usecase_uc5_imola24_undercut.txt, section F); the demo's lap-seek clock gives the same knowledge state and Monte Carlo seed as the full application (docs/evidence/usecase_uc1_monza24_strategy.txt, section A2). The replay also shows what a strategist could not yet know: in UC1 the dashboard learnt of NOR's second stop only at his pit entry, and in UC2 the post-race reading exists only on the last replay frame, while the `End` key gives a reading of the race still running. Because the clock can stop anywhere, the use cases could measure how long each readout stayed on screen (UC3 to UC5) and how a what-if result moves within a lap (UC1).

*Interaction constraints met on the way.* Some interactions change more than the step intends, and the walkthroughs work around them: a double-click focus toggles the selection twice (UC1, step 4); a view opened from the dock becomes a tab of the top-left pane and hides the view there (UC1, steps 10 and 11), and the key `3` unmounts the biplot it shares a pane with (UC2, step 6); at 1536 x 864 the Simulator's verdict row sits below the visible box of the Simulator preset until the splitter is dragged (UC1, step 8); the "pit in k" stepper and the "rivals" toggle keep their values across clock and focus changes (UC5, step 6); and the timeline's pit-stop markers cover the track-status markers of the same lap, so the VSC and SC frames are reached by play and pause (UC4, step 2).

### 5.2 How uncertainty is displayed, and where it is not

Displayed:

- *Probabilities from resampling.* The Circle of Doom gives each rejoin place a probability from 1000 draws of a bootstrap of the race's measured stops, shows the p10–p90 of the pit loss as a hatched arc and in the tooltip, prints the number of stops in the centre, and adds a traffic probability (UC3, UC4, UC5).
- *Intervals.* Race Prediction gives P5–P95 ranges and podium shares, and holds its labels within the Monte Carlo error; the Strategy Simulator gives the ± Monte Carlo error of paired runs on common random numbers and a NO CLEAR CHANGE verdict (UC1). The Cross-Race Analysis panel gives Hedges' g with a 95 % interval, no interval for a single row, and the label "Descriptive differences, not a test" (UC2).
- *Random baselines.* The same panel compares each g with the |g| of random selections of the same size and flags a feature only above a threshold over all twelve features; one and two cars, and the race's 19 cars together, show no feature above random (UC2).
- *Provenance labels.* "prior" on the SC rate (UC1), the "green-flag pit loss and pace" note under neutralisations (UC4), and "≈" on the Strategy Timeline's unmeasured stop losses (UC4).

Not displayed:

- *Time shares.* A readout in a replay holds only until the next lap, pit or track-status event of any car. The shares (for example P6 for 50.0 % of UC3's decision window, or NOR's first VSC readout for 3 % of the frames to his pit entry in UC4) are computed by the scripts; the dashboard shows only the readout of the moment.
- *Model error.* The Simulator's ± is Monte Carlo error only, and the lap model credited fresh hards with about a third of their measured gain (UC1). The rejoin probabilities carry only the pit-loss spread, not pace errors or rival stops that have not started (UC3, UC4, UC5); the backtest of the rejoin place (46.7 % exact, 72.8 % within one place over 709 green stops) is documented but not on screen (UC3). A "100%" label looks the same on one measured stop as on eight; only the stop count, the tooltip's p10–p90 and the arc width tell them apart (UC3).
- *Quantities the views compute but do not show:* the degradation slope's standard error and the reason for "no pit window" (UC1); the predicted crossing times and the gaps behind a rank (UC3, UC4); the flat-model rows and the event record of what moved a readout (UC3); the head-to-head after both stops under a chosen rival stop (UC5); and the dependence of a cross-race reading on the archive (UC2).

### 5.3 Limitations

- *In-sample priors.* The cross-race priors are fitted on all 30 cached races, the race on screen included: the HARD degradation and the SC rate in UC1, the lap model behind "pit in k" in UC3 and UC5. The cross-race view of UC2 fits its axes on, and compares with, an archive that holds the race itself and 14 races run after it; a restricted-archive check, which no view offers, keeps the separation of the groups but changes the panel's labels. The future of the race on screen is not used (UC5, section F of its evidence).
- *Model biases.* The lap model underestimated the gain of fresh hards by a factor of about three at Monza 2024, biasing the Simulator toward the one-stop (UC1). A negative MEDIUM degradation estimate gave the "waiting gains" figure the wrong sign (UC3). Under the SC the view gave VER P3 at 94 % against an actual P1 (UC4). During a stop in progress the stopping car is projected at its old-set pace (UC5). The Circle of Doom holds each car's recent pace constant (UC3 to UC5).
- *Green-flag assumptions under SC and VSC.* The Circle of Doom keeps the green-flag pit loss, paces and reference lap under a neutralisation and says so; stops made under it are not measured and are labelled with the race median; the rank swings as cars complete slow laps, an artefact of the assumption rather than information about the race; and the "pit in k" stepper assumes no safety car (UC4).
- *Readouts that change as cars cross the line.* Race Prediction recomputes within the lap, and a Simulator Run later in the lap gives −1.7 to −2.3 places where the lap-seek frame gives −1.7 (UC1); a Circle of Doom readout can last from a single demo frame (0.8 s) to a whole decision lap (UC3 to UC5); a gap tag reads the latest lap both cars have completed (UC3, UC5). A single screenshot is therefore one state among several, and the use cases report shares.
- *Backend-only parts.* None of the walkthroughs needs the backend. In the full application a double-click focus also requests the lap-time forecast, whose optimal pit lap Tyre Degradation can show as a pit window (UC1); the restricted-archive check of UC2 is an analysis by a backend script, not a view; and several scripts need the git-ignored race cache (section 6).
- *The Undercut Calculator.* The code base holds a view for the head-to-head under a chosen rival stop, but no control opens it. Its numbers in UC5 are analysis only, and the question it answers remains unanswered by the dashboard as it ships.
- *Scope.* Each use case is one race and one or two decisions; the alternatives that were not taken (a second LEC stop, a SAI cover, SAI pitting under the SC) are not observable.
